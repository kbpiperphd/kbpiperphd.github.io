Hello! I am a 3rd year PhD Student in Computational Mechanics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://www.cmu.edu/) in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. My research interests are at the intersection of computational algorithm development and experiment. Recent results include models of thin-film evaporation in porous wicks, which enabled a 42% efficiency improvement in Loop Heat Pipes, predictive models for fracture propagation in ductile functionally graded alloys, and nonlinear reduced order models development with strong mathematical guarantees.

I received my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering from the [California Institute of Technology](https://www.caltech.edu/) in 2024, and my M.S. in Computational Mechanics from Carnegie Mellon University in 2025. While at Caltech, I worked at the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/) studying two-phase thermodynamics, experimental and computational fracture mechanics, and metallurgy. I additionally have collaborated with [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://www.lanl.gov/) on computational fracture mechanics problems, and the [https://arl.devcom.army.mil/](Army Research Laboratory) on reduced order modeling.
